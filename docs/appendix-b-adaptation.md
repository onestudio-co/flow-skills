# Appendix B: Adaptation Guide

> *Optional reading. Find your context and read that section. Each stands alone.*

## Solo Founders

You are all roles. Discovery and Outcome collapse — you discover by building. That's valid.

**Your FLOW**: Cycle Brief (3-field minimum: Hypothesis, Kill Condition, Experiment). One WIP slot, maybe two. G1 Commit is a conversation with yourself: "Is this worth my next 1-3 days?" G3 Resolve is a weekly self-review: kill or keep?

**Kill conditions save you from yourself.** Solo founders are the most vulnerable to Sunk Cost Override. Nobody will tell you to stop. The kill condition is your co-founder who doesn't care about your feelings.

**Agents are your team.** Builder role: the agent codes. Analyst role: compile your metrics. But you still decide.

**Config**: Tempo 1-3 days. WIP 1-2. Rituals: weekly self-review.

## Agencies

The client owns Vision and Strategy. You own Bet and Cycle. The Decision Spine splits across organizational boundaries.

**Discovery as paid deliverable.** A Cycle Brief for Discovery mode is a scoping engagement. Kill conditions reframe as client risk mitigation: "We stop early if the approach isn't working — that protects your investment."

**Per-client WIP, team-level limits.** Four clients does not mean 4x WIP. Your team's WIP limit applies across all clients. At capacity, one client queues.

**G3 with clients.** You can't unilaterally kill what the client paid for. But you can present evidence from the Kill/Merge Record and recommend stopping. Frame kills as fiduciary responsibility, not failure.

**Config**: Tempo per client. WIP per team (not per client). Rituals: per-client cadence + internal weekly triage.

## Hardware

Long observation cycles. Sequential modes — you must validate before manufacturing.

**Modes are strictly sequential.** Discover FIRST, build SECOND. You cannot iterate cheaply on physical products. A killed $5,000 prototype is better than a failed $500,000 manufacturing run.

**The Observation Floor dominates.** Field reliability data takes months. Agents compress design and simulation, but physics and regulators have irreducible timelines.

**Cycle Brief includes cost.** Every experiment has a dollar figure. The kill condition must account for cumulative spend.

**Config**: Tempo weeks-to-months. WIP 1-2 per team. Rituals: monthly Pulse, quarterly Resolve.

## Enterprise (50+)

Multiple teams, portfolio-level WIP. Gates provide governance cover.

**Decision Spine maps to your hierarchy.** Vision (board/exec) -> Strategy (division) -> Bet (squad) -> Cycle (sprint). Map your 6 levels down to 4.

**Gates are governance events.** G1 Commit is investment approval. G3 Resolve is evidence-based portfolio review. Kill conditions have teeth — this is what your PMO has been trying to build.

**Portfolio WIP.** Each squad declares WIP in their FLOW Configuration. Portfolio WIP = sum of squad limits.

**Kill decisions need political cover.** Evidence-based G3 records give PMs data to justify kills. "The kill condition triggered. Here's the data."

**Config**: Tempo per squad. WIP per squad + portfolio cap. Rituals: squad-level Pulse weekly, portfolio Resolve monthly.

---

## Government / Regulated

FLOW gates map to PRINCE2 stage boundaries. Kill conditions are formal project closure.

**Spine maps naturally.** National Vision -> Sector Strategy -> Program (Bet) -> Project Deliverable (Cycle).

**Compliance checkpoints between G1 and G3.** Add regulatory review as a mandatory step. FLOW artifacts become compliance documents with retention requirements.

**Benefits realization built in.** Target metrics in the Cycle Brief are benefits. Post-merge monitoring via Learning Entry is benefits realization tracking.

**Audit trail = Cycle Brief + Kill/Merge Record + Learning Entry.** Three artifacts, complete traceability.

**Config**: Tempo weeks-to-months. WIP per program. Rituals: governance-gated Resolve, monthly portfolio review.
