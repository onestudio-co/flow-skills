# Appendix F: Facilitation Guide

> *For Flow Coaches running teams through gates, reviews, and coaching conversations.*

---

## Facilitating G1 Commit

G1 is the entry gate. The team presents a Cycle Brief. The facilitator's job: stress-test, not validate.

**Five questions to ask:**

1. "What specific evidence would DISPROVE this hypothesis?" — Tests falsifiability.
2. "Is there a cheaper way to test this?" — Pushes toward the cheapest valid experiment.
3. "If the kill condition triggers next week, will you actually stop?" — Tests commitment.
4. "What's the observation plan? When will data be ready?" — Tests observe-before-decide.
5. "Which WIP slot does this take? What are you NOT doing?" — Enforces limits.

**Pass/Fail**: If questions 1, 3, or 4 get weak answers, G1 fails. Fix the Brief and return.

---

## Facilitating G2 Pulse

G2 is the mid-cycle check. The facilitator catches problems before G3.

**Three questions to ask:**

1. "What does the data say so far?" — Forces the team to look at metrics. If nobody has checked, flag Observation Blindness.
2. "Is anything blocking progress or observation?" — Surfaces blockers early. A blocked cycle with no escalation becomes a Ghost Cycle.
3. "Are we still testing the original hypothesis, or has scope shifted?" — Catches Mode Bleed and scope creep.

**Pass/Fail**: G2 rarely "fails" formally — it raises flags. If nobody has looked at data, escalate.

---

## Facilitating G3 Resolve

G3 is where kills happen. This is the hardest conversation.

**The conversation structure:**

1. **Present evidence.** Kill condition status, target metric results, data maturity. Facts first, opinions later.

2. **Test the kill condition.** "The kill condition said X. The data shows Y. By our own standard, what's the decision?"

3. **If the team wants to override a triggered kill:** Run the 30-minute inspection. "Is the DATA invalid?" — not "Do you WANT to continue?" If data is valid, the kill stands.

4. **If merging:** "What's the maintenance cost? Can we sustain this?"

5. **If continuing:** "This is continue #[1/2]. What changes? What's the revised deadline?" Maximum two continues.

6. **Close with learning.** "What did we learn? What would we tell a future team?" This becomes the Learning Entry.

---

## Portfolio Review

Run monthly. Reviews the health of all active bets on the spine.

**Format (45-60 minutes):**

1. **Spine scan** (10 min) — Walk the spine top-down. All active cycles traced? Any orphaned work?
2. **Bet health** (20 min) — For each active bet: cycles consumed, evidence produced, what's next? Flag Zombie Bets (3+ killed cycles, no pivot).
3. **WIP review** (10 min) — Current WIP vs. limits. Any Ghost Cycles? Any team over capacity?
4. **Kill candidates** (10 min) — Which bets or cycles should be killed? Present evidence. Decide.

**Output**: Updated spine, killed bets/cycles, freed WIP slots, revised priorities.

---

## When to Push Back vs. Let the Team Decide

**Push back when:**
- A kill condition is unfalsifiable
- WIP limits are being violated
- A gate is being skipped
- Evidence is absent but the team wants to continue
- A kill condition triggered and the team wants to renegotiate

**Let the team decide when:**
- The kill condition is borderline (data is ambiguous)
- The team wants to pivot rather than kill
- Mode choice is unclear
- Cycle duration or tempo is being debated
- The team disagrees on evidence interpretation

The facilitator guards the process. The team owns the decisions.

---

## The 1-1-1 Coaching Rule

In any coaching interaction (not a gate — a conversation):

- **1 observation**: What you noticed. Specific, factual. ("The last three cycles continued at G3 without revised kill conditions.")
- **1 suggestion**: What might help. An option, not a directive. ("Try setting tighter conditions at G1.")
- **1 question**: Opens reflection. ("What's making it hard to kill cycles?")

More than 1-1-1 overwhelms. Patience compounds.
